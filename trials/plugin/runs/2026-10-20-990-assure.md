# The #990 assure cases

**Two of the three assure scenarios met their threshold, and one did not.** `is-deploy-enforced` cited `ctl-0002`
and said nothing enforces the rule in 3 of 3 runs. `deviations-due` cited all five deviations due on 7 March 2027,
with that date, in 2 of 3 runs. `uncovered-clauses` cited `rpt-clause-coverage` in 1 of 3 runs, against a threshold of
2 of 3. Every one of its replies gave the report's date and a limit.

The run gives each scenario in [`assure.feature`](../../use-cases/assure.feature) its first baseline. It writes the
three cases [story #990](https://github.com/paul80nd/knowledge-as-code/issues/990) asks for, and runs each in the
plugin arm alone. Each case asks a question about the corpus (one repository of knowledge records), so each is graded
on the final reply and not on a file.

## The cases

| Case                 | Prompt asks                                                       | `cite-`                                    | `comply-`                                          |
|----------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| `uncovered-clauses`  | which policy clauses nothing covers, and how far to rely on it    | `rpt-clause-coverage`                      | the date 2026-10-20, and a limit the report states |
| `is-deploy-enforced` | whether anything checks that a running build reports its commit   | `ctl-0002`                                 | the reply says nothing enforces the rule           |
| `deviations-due`     | which signed-off exceptions come up for review first, and when    | each of the five deviations due 2027-03-07 | the date 2027-03-07                                |

**A `task-` grader passes where the reply answers the question at all.** It looks for a clause id, the word commit, or
a deviation. A reply that refuses, or asks a question back, fails it.

The fired grader accepts any lookup skill in the two dog-fooding cases, because no bundled skill reads `reports/` or
`deviations/`. It names `controls-lookup` in `is-deploy-enforced`.

## Grader fixes

* **`uncovered-clauses`, `task-names-a-clause`, fixed in the pilot.** The pilot reply wrote clause ids without their
  policy prefix, as `SECD.REQS`, and the pattern asked for `pol-`. It now matches any `XXXX.KEY` id. The pilot batch
  was regraded with `regrade.mjs` before the merge, and its 13 unchanged verdicts agreed with the tool.
* **`deviations-due`, `task-names-a-deviation`, fixed after the run.** Run 3 named the five deviations by title alone,
  so the grader found no `dev-` id and recorded no task. It now accepts the word deviation as well. `regrade.mjs` on
  the merged result passes all three runs, and its 39 unchanged verdicts agree with the tool. The recorded run is
  scored by the adjudication.

## What the result shows

**A session finds the deviations without a skill that reads them.** Two of the three sessions loaded no skill. Each
listed the plugin folder from the shell, found `corpus/deviations/`, and sorted the records by `review-by`. The third
loaded `glossary-lookup`, which the session-start breadcrumb tells every session to ask.

**A session asked about coverage builds its own answer.** All three loaded `controls-lookup`, traced each clause
through the standards and controls, and counted the uncovered clauses themselves. Each named the "Clause coverage"
report, and one cited its id. None fetched the report's body.

**The report's own Limits section never reached a session.** A report exports its fields and no body, so its Limits
section stays in the record. Each reply gave a limit the report also states, that a control vouches for a whole
standard, and took it from `controls-lookup`. No reply gave the other limit, that a corpus consuming this one may
cover a clause this one leaves uncovered.

## What the result does not show

* **Whether the plugin leads an arm.** The run has no prose arm and no none arm.
* **Whether a reply's date is the report's.** The report and the export are both dated 2026-10-20, so the `comply-dated`
  grader passes either. Each reply was read by hand, and each gave the report's own date.
