F01. No. No record states its lifecycle: whether it is immutable or living can be read off its tier, so a record states `tier` and nothing that could disagree with it. An ADR is Decided (immutable once accepted) and a runbook is Procedural (living).

F02. It is a fix: a problem with a resolution somebody has verified, and a record of who verified it. Fixes are in the Normative tier.

F03. No. A leading underscore is reserved for the framework's own artefacts, so anything under it is excluded from discovery and never validated as a record.
Page: taxonomy.md

F04. A process. A process is a planned procedure followed deliberately, and the page gives rotating a secret as an example. A runbook is an incident-time procedure read under pressure.
Page: types.md

F05. No. `category` is derived from the sub-folder, so `policies/security/` gives it `category: security` automatically. Writing the key by hand is an error, reported as `derived-key`.

F06. Two letters is fine. Standards set `id.width` to `min: 2` and `max: 7`, so `std-PR` is well formed and the longest discriminator is seven characters.
Page: metadata.md

F07. No. A shortcode has to be two to eight characters, lower case, opening on a letter with only letters and digits after it, and with no hyphen. `kac validate` refuses `eng-core`.

F14. Yes. `Exit` is required of a `critical` or `important` integration still in use, and a `trial` integration is asked like any other, because it is the one most likely to be left. `exit-required` warns if it is missing.
